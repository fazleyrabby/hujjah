## 3. What are the conditions of valid tawbah according to the Quran and Sunnah?

- Lang: `en`
- Duration: `5181ms`
- Quran refs: 27:21, 17:77, 35:43, 9:4
- Hadith refs: Sunan al-Kubra (al-Nasa'i) #2769, Sunan al-Kubra (al-Nasa'i) #2770

The conditions for valid tawbah (repentance) in Islam, as established by both the Quran and Hadith, include:

1. Acknowledgment of sinfulness: A person must recognize their sins and acknowledge them.

2. Repentance: They must sincerely repent from those sins.

3. Submission to God's will: The repentant individual should submit to Allah's judgment and forgiveness.

4. Payment of the Fard (obligatory) dues for tawbah: This includes performing Hajj, which is a mandatory act for those who are able to do so. Failure to perform this obligation constitutes valid tawbah.

5. Fulfilling other obligations: If unable to travel for Hajj, one must fulfill the duties of tawbah as laid out by Prophet Muhammad (SAW).

6. Activeness in repentance: The individual should actively seek God's forgiveness and take steps to avoid future sins.

7. Repentance after wrongdoing has occurred: Tawbah is applicable only when a person has committed an evil deed, not before it occurs.

8. No change of the Sunnah (way) of the past people: It must be considered in light of the practices and guidance provided by earlier prophets and their followers.

9. Explicit forgiveness from God: The individual must have received explicit forgiveness from Allah as a result of repentance.

10. Action rather than words alone: While repentance is necessary, it must involve actual action or performance that demonstrates sincerity and commitment to reform.

These conditions ensure that tawbah is considered valid only when accompanied by sincere repentance and the fulfillment of specific obligations prescribed by Islamic law.

## 10. What does Ayat al-Kursi (2:255) mean and why is it so significant?

- Lang: `en`
- Duration: `6233ms`
- Quran refs: 2:255
- Hadith refs: none

The ayah 2:255 from the Quran states:

"God there is no god but He, the Living, the Everlasting. Slumber seizes Him not, neither sleep; to Him belongs all that is in the heavens and the earth. Who is there that shall intercede with Him save by His leave? He knows what lies before them and what is after them, and they comprehend not anything of His knowledge save such as He wills. His Throne comprises the heavens and earth; the preserving of them oppresses Him not; He is the All-high, the All-glorious."

This ayah emphasizes God's omnipotence, omniscience, and absolute control over everything in existence. It highlights that there is no other deity besides God (Allah), who has all knowledge and power.

The significance of this ayah lies in its declaration about God’s attributes: He is the Living, Everlasting, and His presence is everywhere. This establishes Him as the ultimate reality and authority, surpassing human understanding or intercession by any entity.

Moreover, it emphasizes that only with Allah's leave can intercession occur, highlighting divine sovereignty over all aspects of creation. The ayah also underscores God’s knowledge encompassing past, present, and future, further solidifying His omnipotence.

The mention of the throne and its dominance over heaven and earth indicates God's control over both realms, which is a reflection of His ultimate authority over everything in existence. Finally, it highlights that while humans can understand only what Allah wills them to know (about His knowledge), this underscores the absolute nature of God’s knowledge.

In essence, Ayat al-Kursi 2:255 asserts and emphasizes God's perfection by highlighting his omnipotence, omniscience, and control over all aspects of creation. It serves as a foundational belief in Islam that human beings cannot challenge or understand on their own.

## 13. Explain the difference between sabr and shukr and when each is required

- Lang: `en`
- Duration: `5435ms`
- Quran refs: none
- Hadith refs: Sunan al-Kubra (al-Nasa'i) #1304, Jami' at-Tirmidhi #3527

The Quran verses and Hadith provide guidance on the importance of both sabr (patience) and shukr (thankfulness). The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) stressed the need for patience in difficult situations. For instance, he said, "Indeed, part of the bounty's completion is the entrance into Paradise, and salvation from the Fire." This verse highlights that Allah has provided various blessings to ensure people are saved from eternal punishment.

Regarding shukr, the Prophet (peace be upon him) mentioned that it is necessary for individuals to express gratitude through actions. He said, "You have been responded to, so ask." This indicates that there are specific prayers and supplications that a person should make in order to receive blessings or salvation from Hellfire.

The Quran also emphasizes the importance of shukr for making people thankful for what they have received. For example, it states, "Allahumma inni as'aluka at-thabbuta fi al-amr wal-'azimata 'alar-rushdi wa as'aluka shukr ni'matik" which translates to: "O Allah, I ask You for steadfastness in all my affairs and determination in following the right path, I ask You to make me thankful for Your blessings." This verse underscores that thankfulness is essential not just verbally but through actions.

In summary, sabr refers to enduring trials without complaint or anger, while shukr involves expressing gratitude through sincere supplications. Both are critical in achieving salvation and receiving Allah's bounty according to the Quranic guidance.
